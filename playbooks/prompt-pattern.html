The paste-and-hope problem

If you dump raw notes into a chat and hope, you’re leaving half the answer on the table.

Most people treat the model like a search box: paste the mess, hit send, skim whatever comes back. The reply is often polite fluff — a “general summary,” inventing structure you didn’t ask for, or inventing deadlines you never mentioned.

That isn’t a model failure. It’s a briefing failure. The model doesn’t know whether you want a weekly update, a checklist for owners, a rewrite for executives, or a brainstorm. Without constraints and a format, it guesses — and guessing produces text that looks finished but isn’t usable.

This playbook is the long-form companion to our short demo: same messy standup notes, framed once, so you get something you can actually hand off.

The 4-line frame

Four lines above your notes. That’s the whole pattern.

01 · ROLE

Who the model is

Give it a job title for this turn — “concise ops writing assistant,” “skeptical editor,” “meeting scrubber.” Role steers tone and what it prioritizes.

02 · GOAL

What you need done

One sentence on the outcome: turn notes into a weekly update draft, extract open questions, rewrite for a client email. Goal beats “help with this.”

03 · CONSTRAINTS

Limits, tone, what to skip

Max length, no invented deadlines, neutral tone, skip unclear items. Constraints are where quality lives — they stop the model from filling gaps with fiction.

04 · OUTPUT FORMAT

Shape of the answer

Markdown checklist, table, bullets with owners as [TBD], three paragraphs max. Format makes the reply paste-ready instead of “essay-shaped.”

ChatGPT is just the demo surface. The same four lines work in Claude and Gemini. Change the product; keep the briefing.

Copy-paste example

Generic standup notes only — no real names, emails, or company secrets. Paste the frame, then the notes, then send.

Messy notes (input)

standup notes — draft
- follow up on the launch checklist
- need tighter bullets for the weekly update
- someone asked about the timeline?? unclear
- also the FAQ page is messy

4-line prompt frame (paste above the notes)

Role: You are a concise ops writing assistant.
Goal: Turn these standup notes into a usable weekly update draft.
Constraints: Max 5 bullets. No invented deadlines. Neutral tone. Skip anything unclear.
Output format: Markdown checklist with owners left as [TBD].

standup notes — draft
- follow up on the launch checklist
- need tighter bullets for the weekly update
- someone asked about the timeline?? unclear
- also the FAQ page is messy

Expect a short checklist: launch follow-up, weekly bullets, FAQ tidy-up — unclear timeline items omitted or flagged, owners as [TBD], no fake dates. That is “usable,” not “impressive.”

Before / after — what to expect

Qualitative only. No fake metrics, scores, or “X% better” claims.

Raw paste

  • Vague “here’s a general summary…” opening
  • Invented structure or deadlines you never said
  • Essay-length fluff you have to rewrite
  • Unclear items quietly “resolved” by the model

Framed paste

  • Short checklist matching your format line
  • Gaps left as [TBD] or skipped on purpose
  • Tone and length match constraints
  • Something you can paste into a doc and edit

You’re not buying magic. You’re buying a briefing habit: same notes, clearer job for the model, less cleanup for you.

One rule

Memorize this; the rest is optional polish.

Never skip constraints + format.

That’s where quality lives. Role and Goal get you pointed; Constraints and Output format stop fluff, invented facts, and unusable shape. If you’re in a hurry, keep those two lines — always.

When it fails

The frame is not a substitute for judgment. Watch for these.

  1. Vague goal. “Make this better” still leaves the model guessing. Name the artifact (weekly update, FAQ rewrite, open-question list).
  2. Constraints that fight each other. “Max 3 bullets” plus “cover every detail” forces compromise. Pick one priority.
  3. No human check on facts. The frame reduces invention; it doesn’t verify sources. Don’t publish claims you didn’t open yourself.
  4. Wrong job for chat. Recurring templates inside a workspace may belong in Notion (or similar) — use chat for blank-page thinking and one-off framing.
  5. Pasting secrets. Never drop real emails, API keys, or private customer data into a public model. Use generic stand-ins like the demo notes above.
